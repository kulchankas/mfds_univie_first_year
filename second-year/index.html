The courses

250167 VO 6 ECTS · 4 SWS · DSPS
Introduction to Probability Theory
Philipp Christian Petersen · English · Wed & Thu 15:00–16:30 · HS 13, OMP 1 · from 1 Oct

Two halves. Probability — events, conditional probability, independence, random variables, standard distributions, expectation and variance, joint distributions, concentration inequalities, laws of large numbers, the CLT, finite-state Markov chains. Then statistical inference — estimation and likelihood, sampling distributions, bootstrap, confidence intervals, hypothesis and permutation tests, linear regression, model diagnostics, overfitting, cross-validation, binary classification. Theory throughout paired with live Python.

What this timetable implies
  • 22 ECTS across three courses, and the two lecture courses run back-to-back in the same room on four afternoons. There is no clash — but there is also no slack.
  • Tools meets three evenings a week and is assessed continuously, so it will take steady weekly effort rather than an exam-week sprint. Its midterm coding exam is fixed for 24 Nov.
  • Probability and Optimisation are both single written exams at the end. That means the pattern you already know from first year: nothing forces you to keep up, so the risk is arriving at January having attended everything and retained little. The countermeasure is the same one that works now — flashcards from week 1, not week 12.

What to do before October

Highest-value preparation, in order
  1. Probability. Genuinely worth starting early — it assumes almost nothing from first year, so you can get ahead without gaps. The course page has a free-textbook route and a six-week head-start plan.
  2. Python fluency. All three courses use it; Probability has live Python demos and Tools spends weeks on it. If your Python is rusty this is the cheapest thing to fix over the summer.
  3. Optimisation needs no new preparation — it continues your Analysis material. Keeping the Analysis deck alive through the break is the preparation.
