Sheet 2 — Master Theorem, polynomial growth & Akra–Bazzi

problems 18–29 · file ss26_algodat-2.pdf · open PDF
The correction that matters The sheet opens with an erratum: the lecture book states the polynomial growth condition with the quantifiers in the wrong order. The correct form is $$\forall \varphi>1\ \exists d>1\ \exists N>0\ \forall n\ge N\ \forall 1\le\psi\le\varphi:\ \tfrac1d f(n)\le f(\psi n)\le d\,f(n).$$ Note $\exists N$ comes after $\forall\varphi$. This condition is the hypothesis Akra–Bazzi needs, and four problems (22–25) are about it — see the drills page for the worked treatment.
#TopicWhat it tests
18 ★MasterFive recurrences $T(n)=2T(n/4)+f$ with $f=\Theta(1),\Theta(\sqrt n),\Theta(\sqrt n\lg^2 n),\Theta(n),\Theta(n^2)$ — one per Master case, including the $\lg^k$ variants. Exam Q2 is exactly this. → drills B
19MasterProf. Caesar's matrix multiplication: largest $a$ with $aT(n/4)+\Theta(n^2)$ beating Strassen; then for $n/3$, $n/5$. Inverting the Master Theorem.
20–21 ★regularityTwo counterexamples showing the regularity condition is not automatic: $f(n)=\lg n$ with $a=1,b=2$; and $f(n)=2^{\lceil\lg n\rceil}$ meeting every case-3 condition except regularity. If you ever claim case 3 without checking regularity, these are why.
22–25 ★poly growthThe polynomial growth condition: $n^2$ satisfies it, $2^n$ doesn't (22); an equivalent formulation with $u\in[\varphi n,n]$ (23); $n\lg n$ and $n\lg\lg n$ satisfy it (24); $f(\Theta(n))=\Theta(f(n))$ and why that notation is legitimate (25).
26 ★Akra–BazziFive Akra–Bazzi computations incl. $T(n/2)+T(n/3)+T(n/6)+\Theta(n\lg n)$ and $3T(n/3)+8T(n/4)+\Theta(n^2/\lg n)$. → drills C
27Akra–BazziUse Akra–Bazzi to re-prove the continuous Master Theorem. A proof question, and a good one to have seen.
28–29 ★change of vars$T(n)=2T(\sqrt n)+\Theta(\lg n)$ via $m=\lg n$, $S(m)=T(2^m)$; then three more of the same shape. A technique with its own dedicated problems — expect it. → drills D

30binomials$\binom{2n}{n}=\frac{2^{2n}}{\sqrt{\pi n}}(1+O(1/n))$ and $\binom nk\ge \frac{n^k}{4k!}$ for $k\le\sqrt n$. Estimation practice feeding the randomised analyses later.

Sheet 5 — expected running times & a real scheduler

problems 44–51 · file ss26_algodat-5.pdf (header says "Sheet 4") · open PDF
#TopicWhat it tests
44–46 ★expected time JuliaThree searches of an unsorted array — random (sampling with replacement), deterministic (linear scan), scramble (shuffle then scan) — each analysed for 1 match, $k$ matches, 0 matches. The random-search answers are geometric: $n$, $n/k$, and the coupon-collector-flavoured $n H_n$ for the exhaustive case. Then: which would you actually use? A superb expected-value workout.
47–49priority queue JuliaBuild a heap, then a priority queue with handles, then a Linux-style process scheduler running 1000 iterations. The handles requirement is the interesting part — that's what makes DECREASE-KEY usable in practice.
50quicksortCalls to RANDOM in randomised quicksort: $\Theta(n)$ both best and worst case (one per partition).
51 ★correctness recurrenceStooge sort — argue correctness, then $T(n)=3T(2n/3)+\Theta(1)=\Theta(n^{\log_{1.5}3})\approx\Theta(n^{2.71})$. A correctness proof and a Master-Theorem application in one problem; very exam-shaped.

#TopicWhat it tests
52–53stacks/queuesQueue from two stacks (amortised $O(1)$ per op) and stack from two queues. The amortised argument in 52 is the one piece of amortised analysis the sheets ask for directly.
54–55linked listsWhy singly-linked INSERT is $\Theta(1)$ but DELETE is $\Theta(n)$; then reverse a list in $\Theta(n)$ with $O(1)$ extra space.
56–58 ★tree walksPrint an $n$-node binary tree three ways: recursive post-order, non-recursive with an explicit stack, and non-recursive in $O(1)$ extra space without modifying the tree. The third is genuinely hard (Morris-style / parent-pointer walk) and a great "describe an algorithm" candidate.

63BSTWrite TREE-PREDECESSOR (mirror of successor). → notes
64–67BSTProf. Kilmer's false claim $a\le b\le c$ across a search path (find the smallest counterexample); leaf/parent key relation; when TRANSPLANT's $v$ is NIL; whether deletion is commutative (it is not — a classic).
68–69 ★red-blackLargest/smallest number of internal nodes at black-height $k$ ($2^{2k}-1$ and $2^k-1$); the extremal red:black ratios. Directly supports exam Q4(b).

What the sheets tell you about the exam
  • Loop invariants are drilled from day one (problems 2, 4, 6) — and duly appeared as Q1. Selection sort (5–7) is the most likely rotation.
  • Recurrences get a whole sheet plus half of sheet 1 — 20-odd problems. Q2 will keep appearing.
  • Two topics are heavily examined on the sheets but were absent from the first paper: dynamic programming (sheets 9–10, nineteen problems) and probability (sheet 3 + sheets 4–5). If the second-date paper rotates, these are where it will rotate to.
  • AVL trees (80) and graph traversal (101–102) are the two topics with no coverage on this site yet — see the plan on the Algorithms hub.
  • Problems marked Julia want an implementation; the exam is written, so treat them as pseudocode practice unless your lecturer says otherwise.
