Recurrence drills

Exam question 2 is always: "give asymptotically tight upper and lower bounds" — i.e. a justified $\Theta$. All four recurrences on the first-date paper were the case-2 / log-factor family, including one the vanilla Master Theorem can't solve. This page: the decision procedure, then 21 drills.

The decision procedure

For $T(n)=a\,T(n/b)+f(n)$ with $a\ge1$, $b>1$: compute the watershed $W(n)=n^{\log_b a}$ and compare $f$ against it.

SituationCaseAnswer
$f(n)=O\big(n^{\log_b a-\varepsilon}\big)$ — polynomially smaller1$T=\Theta\big(n^{\log_b a}\big)$ (leaves dominate)
$f(n)=\Theta\big(n^{\log_b a}\lg^k n\big)$, $k>-1$2 (extended)$T=\Theta\big(n^{\log_b a}\lg^{k+1} n\big)$ (all levels equal)
$f(n)=\Theta\big(n^{\log_b a}\lg^{-1} n\big)$  ($k=-1$)2*$T=\Theta\big(n^{\log_b a}\lg\lg n\big)$
$f(n)=\Theta\big(n^{\log_b a}\lg^{k} n\big)$, $k<-1$2*$T=\Theta\big(n^{\log_b a}\big)$ (the level sum converges)
$f(n)=\Omega\big(n^{\log_b a+\varepsilon}\big)$ — polynomially larger, and regularity $a f(n/b)\le c f(n)$, $c<1$3$T=\Theta\big(f(n)\big)$ (root dominates)
  • Polynomially smaller/larger means by a factor $n^{\varepsilon}$ — a $\lg$ factor is not enough for cases 1/3. That's exactly when the $k$-rows apply.
  • Claiming case 3? Check regularity explicitly: for $f(n)=n^s\lg^t n$ with $s>\log_b a$ it always holds ($af(n/b)\approx (a/b^s)f(n)$, $a/b^s<1$) — but write the one line.
  • Nothing fits (unequal splits, $T(n-1)$, $T(\sqrt n)$)? → Akra–Bazzi, direct expansion, or substitution — see below.
Akra–Bazzi in one box (full statement → proofs page) For $T(x)=g(x)+\sum_i a_iT(b_ix)$: solve $\;\sum_i a_ib_i^{\,p}=1\;$ for $p$, then $T(x)=\Theta\!\Big(x^p\big(1+\int_{1}^{x} g(u)\,u^{-p-1}\,du\big)\Big)$. Standard integrals: $\int\!\frac{du}{u}=\ln u$, $\;\int\!\frac{du}{u\ln u}=\ln\ln u$, $\;\int u^{s}\,du=\frac{u^{s+1}}{s+1}$ ($s\neq-1$).
The other two tools Recursion tree — draw levels, sum level costs; how the Master Theorem is proved (Lemmas 4.2/4.3) and the honest fallback for anything odd. Substitution — guess the bound, prove it by induction with explicit constants; the rigorous way to verify a tight bound (used in Thm 9.2).
Exam habit Every answer = three visible steps: ① $a$, $b$, $W(n)=n^{\log_b a}$ computed; ② the comparison ("exact match with $k=1$", "polynomially smaller"); ③ the case named + the $\Theta$ answer. For case 3 add the regularity line. Two points each on the real paper — don't donate them to speed.

Drill bank

Groups A→D get harder. Do them cold, then check. EXAM 1 marks the actual first-date recurrences.

A — the three cases, warm-up

A1 $T(n)=2T(n/2)+n$
$W=n^{\log_2 2}=n$; $f=n=\Theta(n\lg^0 n)$ — exact match, case 2 ($k=0$). $\Theta(n\lg n)$ — merge sort.
A2 $T(n)=8T(n/2)+n^2$
$W=n^{\log_2 8}=n^3$; $f=n^2=O(n^{3-\varepsilon})$ — polynomially smaller, case 1. $\Theta(n^3)$.
A3 $T(n)=2T(n/2)+n^3$
$W=n$; $f=n^3=\Omega(n^{1+\varepsilon})$ — case 3. Regularity: $2f(n/2)=2\cdot n^3/8=n^3/4\le\frac14 f(n)$ ✓. $\Theta(n^3)$.
A4 $T(n)=T(n/2)+\Theta(1)$
$W=n^{\log_2 1}=n^0=1$; $f=\Theta(1)$ — exact match, case 2 ($k=0$). $\Theta(\lg n)$ — binary search.
A5 $T(n)=7T(n/2)+n^2$
$W=n^{\log_2 7}\approx n^{2.807}$; $f=n^2$ polynomially smaller — case 1. $\Theta(n^{\log_2 7})$ — Strassen's matrix multiplication.
A6 $T(n)=3T(n/2)+n$
$W=n^{\log_2 3}\approx n^{1.585}$; $f=n$ polynomially smaller — case 1. $\Theta(n^{\log_2 3})$ — Karatsuba multiplication.
A7 $T(n)=3T(n/4)+n\lg n$
$W=n^{\log_4 3}\approx n^{0.793}$; $f=n\lg n=\Omega(n^{0.793+\varepsilon})$ — case 3. Regularity: $3f(n/4)=\frac34 n(\lg n-2)\le\frac34 f(n)$ ✓. $\Theta(n\lg n)$.

B — the case-2 family (what exam 1 actually was)

B1 $T(n)=16T(n/4)+n^2$ EXAM 1
$W=n^{\log_4 16}=n^2$ — exact match ($k=0$), case 2. $\Theta(n^2\lg n)$.
B2 $T(n)=4T(n/2)+n^2\lg n$ EXAM 1
$W=n^{\log_2 4}=n^2$; $f=\Theta(n^2\lg^1 n)$ — extended case 2, $k=1$. $\Theta(n^2\lg^2 n)$.
B3 $T(n)=2T(n/4)+\sqrt n$ EXAM 1
$W=n^{\log_4 2}=n^{1/2}$ — exact match ($k=0$), case 2. $\Theta(\sqrt n\,\lg n)$.
B4 $T(n)=2T(n/2)+n\lg n$
$W=n$; $f=\Theta(n\lg^1 n)$ — $k=1$. $\Theta(n\lg^2 n)$.
B5 $T(n)=9T(n/3)+n^2\lg^2 n$
$W=n^{\log_3 9}=n^2$; $k=2$. $\Theta(n^2\lg^3 n)$.
B6 $T(n)=4T(n/2)+\dfrac{n^2}{\lg n}$
$W=n^2$; $f=\Theta(n^2\lg^{-1}n)$ — the $k=-1$ row. $\Theta(n^2\lg\lg n)$.

C — beyond the vanilla theorem

C1 $T(n)=3T(n/3)+\dfrac{n}{\lg n}$ EXAM 1
$W=n$; $f=\Theta(n\lg^{-1}n)$: cases 1–3 all fail ($f$ is smaller than $W$ but not polynomially). The $k=-1$ row gives $\Theta(n\lg\lg n)$. Akra–Bazzi check: $p=1$, $\int_2^n\frac{du}{u\lg u}=\Theta(\lg\lg n)$ ✓.
C2 $T(n)=2T(n/2)+\dfrac{n}{\lg^2 n}$
$W=n$; $k=-2<-1$ — the level sum $\sum_j 1/j^2$ converges, so the driving costs add only $O(n)$. $\Theta(n)$.
C3 $T(n)=T(n/3)+T(2n/3)+n$
Unequal split — Akra–Bazzi. $(1/3)^p+(2/3)^p=1\Rightarrow p=1$; $\int_1^n u\cdot u^{-2}du=\ln n$. $\Theta(n\lg n)$. (Recursion-tree view: every level costs $n$, depth $\Theta(\lg n)$.)
C4 $T(n)=T(n/5)+T(7n/10)+n$
The SELECT recurrence. At $p=1$: $\frac15+\frac{7}{10}=0.9<1$, so the balancing $p<1$. Then $\int_1^n u^{-p}du=\Theta(n^{1-p})$ and $T=\Theta(n^p\cdot n^{1-p})$. $\Theta(n)$ — the sum of subproblem sizes is $<n$, so the top level dominates.
C5 $T(n)=2T(n/2)+\dfrac{n^2}{\lg n}$
$W=n$; $f=n^2/\lg n=\Omega(n^{1+\varepsilon})$ (e.g. $\ge n^{1.5}$ eventually) — case 3. Regularity: $2f(n/2)=\frac{n^2/2}{\lg n-1}\le c\,\frac{n^2}{\lg n}$ with $c\to\frac12$ ✓. $\Theta\!\big(n^2/\lg n\big)$ — case 3 answers $\Theta(f)$, log factor and all.

D — not Master-shaped at all

D1 $T(n)=T(n-1)+n$
Subtractive, not divide-and-conquer: expand. $T(n)=\sum_{k=1}^n k+O(1)$. $\Theta(n^2)$ — worst-case quicksort.
D2 $T(n)=2T(n-1)+1$
Expand: $T(n)=2^n T(0)+2^{n-1}+\cdots+1$. $\Theta(2^n)$ — Tower of Hanoi.
D3 $T(n)=T(\sqrt n)+1$
Substitute $m=\lg n$, $S(m)=T(2^m)$: then $S(m)=S(m/2)+1=\Theta(\lg m)$. $\Theta(\lg\lg n)$.
Self-test Redo B1–C1 in under 8 minutes total — that's the exam pace (2 pts each, four parts in one question). If any group-C item needs more than 3 minutes, reread the decision table and try again tomorrow; they're also in the flashcards.
