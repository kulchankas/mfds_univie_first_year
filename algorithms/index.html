⭐ Exam-priority proofs

The proofs the professor flagged as exam-relevant. Master these end-to-end.

General proof techniques
  • Loop invariants — prove correctness by initialization, maintenance, termination. EXAM 1 · Q1, 8 pts — bubblesort, worked on the mock page; template + insertion sort on the proofs page.
  • Master Theorem — recurrences $T(n)=a\,T(n/b)+f(n)$ via $f$ vs. $n^{\log_b a}$. EXAM 1 · Q2, 8 pts — all four parts were the case-2/log-factor family: drill bank.
  • Akra–Bazzi theorem — the general method when the Master Theorem fails (unequal splits, $n/\lg n$ driving functions). EXAM 1 · Q2b — statement & recipe on the proofs page.

Lemma 5.4 — Ch. 5, probabilistic analysis & randomized algorithms

Self-study & exam preparation

How to prepare — the short version
  • Calibrate first: skim the real first-date paper. The format is fixed: correctness-by-invariant + four recurrences + two "describe a data structure" questions + one big proof (Huffman was 10 pts).
  • Then the two passes: content (notes + drills) → proofs from memory → timed mocks. The full schedule, interleaved with Analysis and Linear Algebra, is the 6-week plan.
  • Daily: the flashcard deck (20 min) — definitions, runtimes, Master cases, fixup cases.
  • Rotation risk: flagged proofs that didn't appear on the first date (quicksort, lower bound, selection, hashing, augmentation) are prime candidates for the second — they're all on the proofs page.
